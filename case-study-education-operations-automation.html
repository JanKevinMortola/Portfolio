Building a Connected Teacher, Session & Attendance Operations System

This case study is anonymized to protect client and participant information.

An education organization needed a more dependable way to coordinate schools, teacher assignments, scheduled sessions, attendance, exceptions, reminders, purchase-order delivery, and director-facing operational visibility. The challenge was not one isolated automation; it was the number of records and relationships that had to stay consistent as day-to-day operations changed.

The operational problem

Important information lived across multiple records and workflows. Re-entering the same teacher, school, session, and contract information increased the risk of mismatches. Attendance needed to be captured in a controlled way, missed actions needed escalation, and managers needed to see operational exceptions without manually checking every underlying record.

System architecture

I structured the operating model around master records and relationships instead of duplicating the same data in every workflow. Core entities included school records, contract or purchase-order records, teacher assignments, session records, daily attendance, and exception records.

The automation layer used n8n to connect the operating data and trigger reminders, submissions, status updates, exception handling, and reporting logic. Google Apps Script and Google Workspace components were used where a lightweight form or supporting internal tool was more practical than adding another platform.

Key workflow patterns

Teacher session and attendance workflow

Teachers could submit session actions through a controlled portal or form. The workflow resolved the correct session and teacher records, captured clock-in or clock-out activity, and updated the linked operational records.

Reminder and escalation logic

Scheduled reminders supported expected teacher actions. When an expected action was not received, the workflow could escalate the issue and create or update an operational exception instead of allowing a missed event to disappear silently.

Exception-first management

Late, missed, incomplete, or otherwise unusual attendance events were separated from normal operations so management could focus on records that actually needed attention.

Contract and delivery visibility

Session delivery data was designed to connect back to contract or purchase-order tracking so operational activity and financial coverage could be viewed together.

Why this approach matters

The value of the system is not simply that a reminder can be sent automatically. The larger benefit is that each operational event contributes to a connected source of truth. Teachers interact with a simple front end, while the underlying relationships, exception handling, and reporting logic remain structured for the operations team.
